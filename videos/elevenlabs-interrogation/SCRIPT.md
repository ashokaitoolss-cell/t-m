# Who's making you say this?

A detective plays a suspect his own recorded threats. The suspect insists he
"didn't have a choice." Every tape is a different emotion or language, and he
reproduces each one on demand. In the end we learn who wrote the words: the
detective. Same face. His case file holds the suspect's whole performance,
written in the voice editor with emotion tags.

The detective's face stays hidden until the reveal. He has been directing all
along, so his "interrogation" lines are really direction notes ("No. The way
you said it.").

## Case file insert

Printed page clipped inside the folder, styled like the voice editor:
speaker chip, then each line with its emotion tags. Source and print-ready
files: `props/case-file-page.html`, `props/case-file-page.pdf` (A4),
`props/case-file-page.png` (preview).

```
( ● Arjun - My Voice )

[calm] [menacing] I know where you sleep.
[scared] Sir, please... [gulps] I didn't have a choice.
[nervous] [stammering] I... I know where you... sleep.
[confused] I don't even speak—
[whispers] आज रात दरवाज़ा बंद करना मत भूलना।
[crying] Please... please, I'm begging you. [sobs] Just let me go home.
[exhausted] I only said what I was told.
[short pause] You did.
[deadpan] You asked for one.

—————— + Add speaker ——————
```

- "Arjun" is a placeholder: use the actor's or character's name. "My Voice"
  on the chip is what makes the connection click instantly.
- The same line appearing twice with different tags (`[menacing]` vs
  `[nervous] [stammering]`) is the payoff of "No. The way you said it."
- Frame the insert so it ends on "You did." The last line, "You asked for
  one.", stays below frame so it isn't read before it's spoken. It's on the
  page as an Easter egg for people who pause on a rewatch.

## Props

- **Walkman:** a blue and silver TPS-L2-style Sony Walkman, no headphones.
  Three cassettes with evidence stickers (EXHIBIT A, B, C). Each swap gives a
  door *clack* that marks the beat change.
- **Case file:** a manila folder that sits closed on the table from the first
  wide shot, so opening it later feels set up. The cover can optionally read
  "CASE NO. 11" as a nod to the brand.

## Notes

- The original Walkman has no speaker. Viewers won't question it; for an
  airtight version, run a short cable to a small speaker just out of frame.
- If this runs as an official ElevenLabs ad, cover the SONY / WALKMAN
  lettering or angle it away from camera (third-party trademark).
- Burn in captions for every line. Many people watch Reels muted, and the
  hook ("I know where you sleep.") has to land on frame one without sound.
